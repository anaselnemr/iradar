01What happens on a check

{#- NOT |capitalize: it lowercases the rest, so "every Monday" comes out "Every monday". Starting the sentence with a word of our own instead. -#}

Runs {{ every }} at {{ at_time }}, on its own, on iRadar’s own server: nobody starts it and no laptop has to stay on{% if run_hours %}. It takes about {{ run_hours }}{% endif %}.

  1. Every page is opened in a real browser

    Not downloaded as code, but rendered the way you would see it. It scrolls to the bottom so anything that loads late appears, opens tabs and accordions, waits for the images, and reads the buy box only once it has stopped changing. Prices are always read in US dollars, so a page never “changes” because of the visitor’s currency.

    A page that only half-loads (most of its sections or its library missing) is retried rather than saved.
  2. What is on it is written down

    The visible text, every image, the whole library (documents, videos, boards, design resources, partners), the parametrics, the product status, the price, the buy buttons and a full-page screenshot.

  3. It is compared with last time

    Six signals, below. Any one of them is enough to raise the page.

  4. Anything raised goes to a vote

    Before the run ends, the page is captured again, and the two captures must agree on what changed, not merely that something did. If they agree, the update is published. If they disagree, a third capture breaks the tie. If the second capture shows nothing, the update is dropped silently.

    A page that LOST content is judged harder: unanimous when the first two agree, because a half-loaded page looks exactly like a page that lost half its content.
  5. Pages that fail get two more tries

    Retried at the end of the run. Only something that fails every attempt is recorded as an error. One bad moment is not a verdict.

  6. What survives lands in your queue

    On the Dashboard, marked Updated. Nothing is altered or deleted. The previous version stays until you decide.

The bar you watch while it runs

While a check is going, the Dashboard shows one bar with every page in it. Each page sits in exactly one colour, and the five always add up to the total, so the bar answers “how far along is it” without you reading any numbers.

{#- a replica, not a screenshot: a picture of the bar is out of date the first time the colours change, and would be wrong in one of the two themes no matter which one it was taken in -#}
  • No update: captured, compared, nothing moved. On a normal check this is nearly the whole bar.
  • Updated: confirmed. It survived the second capture and is in your queue.
  • Reconfirming updates: the page looked different and is being held for a second capture. Not an update yet. These are only settled at the very end of the check, so this number climbs all the way through and then empties in one go, most of it into no update.
  • Blocked, will retry: did not load this time. Every one gets two more tries at the end, after a pause, and even failing all three only counts a strike. A page is written down as a Capture error only after it has failed every attempt in two checks in a row (six tries in all), so this number usually falls near the end rather than rising.
  • Waiting: not reached yet.

A big reconfirming updates number part way through is normal. It does not mean hundreds of updates are waiting for you. It is the holding pen, and the second capture is what empties it.

Starting one yourself

Check all pages runs everything now. The arrow beside it offers partial runs, such as only the pages never captured. Tick Send report first if you want this manual check to email its report as well, which the scheduled one always does. To re-read a handful of pages, tick them in the list and choose Capture.

If a check stops half way

It picks up where it left off. Every page is recorded as it is done, so a check that is interrupted (the server restarts or redeploys) resumes from the first page it had not reached, rather than starting again.

  • Pages already checked are not re-checked. Their results stand.
  • Pages caught mid-capture are simply redone. A half-written capture is never kept, so an interruption cannot leave a corrupted reference behind.
  • Updates already found are still in your queue. Nothing waiting for review is lost.
  • It starts itself again. You do not have to notice, and you do not have to press anything.

The only way a check stops for good is if you stop it. Pause holds it where it is, and End check finishes it and keeps every page already captured.

Page states you will see

Under Status on the Dashboard, and as the chips under the search box. Each one is a filter.

  • Unchanged: checked, nothing moved. The normal state.
  • Updated: something changed in the latest check.
  • Awaiting review: every page with an update nobody has decided yet, whatever its status is now. This is your real queue.
  • Recently offline: the address answers 404 or 410. The page is gone, or was never published. See Offline, paused, archived.
  • Paused: deliberately not checked. Still listed.
  • Blank page: the address answered but there was nothing on it. Usually Infineon serving an empty shell. It is retried next check.
  • Capture error: it failed every attempt in two checks in a row, six tries in all. Almost always the site refusing us, not the page being broken.
  • Not captured: added but never read yet, so there is nothing to compare against. It gets its first reading next check.
  • Ownership check: the page’s BL or PL could not be confirmed. See Who owns a page.

02What it notices, and what it lets pass

The second list, ignored on purpose, is why something you expected did not come up. Each one caused a flood of false alarms before it was handled.

✓ Raises a page

  • TextWording added, removed or edited, including the description, parametrics, product status and price. Text that only moved does not count: same lines, new order, is layout.
  • LibraryA document, video, board, design resource or partner appears or disappears, or its version, date, size or price changes.
  • ImagesA picture appears on or disappears from the page.
  • ScreenshotMore than {{ s.visual_threshold }}% of pixels differ, or the page height moves more than {{ s.height_threshold }}px.
  • Products tableThe table of parts changes shape: the product count the page itself reports moves, or a column appears or disappears. Columns dealt in a new order do not count.
  • File updatesA file republished at the same address. The page is identical, but the datasheet behind it is new.

✕ Ignored on purpose

  • Stock levels“4387 in stock” reads 4382 by morning. The price is still watched.
  • The community feedRe-sorts itself constantly. Whether the widget is present, empty or busy is tracked.
  • Latest news, the mega-menuIdentical on every page. One retired thumbnail once raised 585 pages at once.
  • Tabs loading differentlyTwo visits can land on different tabs. Their contents are tracked item by item instead.
  • Timestamps, “3 days ago”They change with nobody editing anything.
  • The same words, set differentlyText that wraps at a different word, a space added or dropped, an HTML code instead of the character it stands for. Any changed word still counts.
  • The same values in a new orderA list or parameter whose values are only dealt differently.
  • The same picture at a new sizeThe size travels in the image’s address, so one photo can arrive under several. A different picture still counts.

03Reviewing an update

Open one from the Dashboard. It names what moved, component by component (page text · Related products (+1/-1) · images (+2/-2)), then shows each part: the wording before and after, which items came and went, and each changed area up close, before on the left and after on the right with the change outlined in amber. The full screenshots underneath carry the same areas boxed in red.

What earns a red box, and what deliberately does not. A box means the pixels are different and the difference survived a battery of honesty checks. Things a reader cannot see are never boxed: text re-rendered a hair differently, a row that slipped a pixel when the page reflowed, a block that merely moved. A block that moved and changed inside is still boxed, and a section that was added or removed always is. If an update says TEXT but shows no boxes, the change sits in a collapsed tab or accordion, and the page says so where the boxes would have been.

Approve

The new version is correct. It becomes the reference the next check compares against.

Use when the change is real, whether it matters or not.

Reject

The old version was correct. It stays the reference.

Use when the capture is wrong, or the change will be undone.

Two reasons to reject

  • The capture is wrong. Something did not load and iRadar saw half a page. Next week it captures properly, matches the version you kept, and says nothing.
  • The change is real but wrong, and will be put back. Somebody published a mistake. Reject it, tell whoever owns the page, and the reference stays on the correct version, so when they fix it the page matches again and goes quiet on its own.
    Until it is fixed the page comes up again on every check. That is the point: it is a standing reminder that the site still does not say what it should.

Approve is the normal one. Approving does not endorse anything. It only records what the page says today. Every decision records who made it and when, shown on the update.

Many at once: tick pages on the Dashboard (or Select all to take every page the filters match, not just the ones on screen) and choose Approve updates.

04Finding pages on the Dashboard

One search box and a row of filters. Every option shows how many pages it would give you before you tick it, and the list follows as you tick, with no Apply button to press.

  • Search Name, title or address. Part and board names can be typed with spaces instead of dashes: SLE 78CFX1M10PH finds SLE-78CFX1M10PH.
  • Type The page type, and the level under it (PTP, EB, PFP and so on). It comes first because it decides which other filters mean anything.
  • Ownership Business line, product line, and the segment for application pages. Only values that apply to the pages in view are offered, and (not assigned) finds the gaps.
  • PTP / EB Locked until you pick part pages or boards, with the switch at the top. Then three things only those pages have: the Perfect page standard, the product status (Active and preferred, Not for new design, Discontinued and the rest) and the buy button: whether there is one, and exactly which buttons the box shows (Add to cart, Contact sales, View distributor’s inventory and their combinations).
  • Status The page states, and the way into the Archive.
  • Content & components What is on the page. Content is the library (documents, videos by where they sit, images, a linked eval board) plus over its limit for copy that breaks the length rules. Components are the page’s own blocks: subheadline, key features, benefits, parametrics, the Design resources tabs, metadata, primary document, package page link and more. Each has Has, No and, where it applies, Empty.
  • Views Save the filters in force under a name. Views are shared, so everyone on the team sees the same list.

Doing things to many pages

Tick pages, or Select all for every page the filters match, then choose an action: Capture (re-read them now), Approve updates, Pause, Resume, Archive, Export or Remove.

06Charts: how complete each page type is

Explore → Charts, with a tab for PTPs and one for EBs. The numbers are worked out live from the latest captures.

  • Overview: perfect pages by business line and by product line, then each key component as has against missing.
  • Heatmap: components down, product lines across, grouped by business line. Each cell gives the pages and the percentage, so the gaps show as pale cells.
  • Drill-down: tick business lines, product lines or components and compare just those.

The filters card

Cluster narrows to a group of business lines and ticks its extra components (SURF adds PCB design data). Components is the standard being measured, from the key set to all of them. Scope picks business and product lines. Pages chooses online, offline or both, and Product status and Buy button narrow it further. The line at the top of the card says what is in force while it is collapsed.

Every chart downloads as PNG or SVG, and a chart’s title can be clicked and retyped before you download it, for a slide.

07Who owns a page

BL, PL, CM and the marketing owner. Shown on every page, filterable on the Dashboard, scoped on the Charts and available as export columns.

  • Part pages and boards fill themselves. Their PL comes from PIM, as infineon.com shows it on the page, and the BL from the ES org chart. A run fills an empty one, and follows the page when infineon.com changes it. The small PIM tag beside the PL says where it came from.
  • A PL set by hand stays. Edit it with the pencil and it is tagged set by hand. No run changes it after that.
  • Nothing is guessed. A page with no source for its BL or PL, or whose PL is not on the current ES chart, is flagged Ownership check for a person to settle. Pages taken offline and document pages never carry the flag.
  • Application pages carry a segment (C3, A, I2 and their combinations), editable on the page and filterable beside BL and PL.
  • Product lines read with their names: 92 (MCU), 44 (ENV), everywhere a PL appears.

Ownership is our bookkeeping, not the page’s content, so editing it never raises an update.

08Content: every document, and where it is used

Every check reads the whole library off each page, so Content is not a separate list somebody maintains. It is what the site actually says today.

It is organised by the thing, not by the page. One datasheet used on forty product pages is one entry with forty pages under it. Open it and you get:

  • Which pages carry this document: every one, with its type and the heading it sits under on that page
  • Its details: version, language, date, size, and whether it is public or behind a MyInfineon login
  • A picture of the first page, so you can tell whether it is the right file without downloading it
  • Where to edit it: the DAM and Windchill badges go straight to the source system{% if is_admin %} (an admin sets those links under Settings → Advanced){% endif %}

This is what makes a removal visible. When a datasheet disappears from a page, iRadar knows because it compares the whole library, not just the words. That is the Library signal.

File updates: the other queue

A datasheet can be replaced without the page moving at all: same link, same title, new file behind it. iRadar asks each file for its size and date without downloading it, and when those move it goes to File updates.

  • Its own queue, with To review and Reviewed tabs, a search and Documents / Images chips, separate from page updates so a quiet republish does not get lost among them.
  • Every page carrying that file is listed. One replaced datasheet can be forty pages’ worth of news.
  • Approve records the new file as the one we know about. Reject keeps the old reading, for when the server is reporting nonsense rather than the file genuinely changing.

09What a page’s own page shows

Click any row on the Dashboard for everything iRadar knows about that one page.

  • Where it sits: the breadcrumb, the way Infineon nests it. The counter beside it opens the pages beside this one and under it.
  • Its name, address and badges: AEM opens the page in the author editor, PIM opens a part or board in PIM.
  • Ownership: BL, PL with its source tag, CM, owner, and the segment for application pages. All editable with the pencil.
  • Components, product status, price and buy button, as the last capture read them.
  • The library: subheadline, description, parametrics, features, documents, design resources, videos, images and the rest, each opening to its items.
  • Sentiment analysis: recent mentions of the product family across the web, where there are any.
  • Detected updates: every change ever raised on this page, with its verdict. This is the page’s history.
  • Stored snapshots, Redirects when the address that answered is not the one on the list, and Notes at the bottom.

10Offline, paused, archived

Three different reasons a page is not simply live and checked, kept apart on purpose.

  • Recently offline infineon.com answers 404 or 410. The page stays in the list, is still looked at on every check (pages do come back), and drops its ownership flag, since a gone page needs no owner. Pausing it is a person’s call, never automatic.
  • Paused Set aside by somebody, for a page known to be in flux. Still in the inventory and every count, just not checked. Resume brings it back. Document pages (a gated file rather than a page) are paused automatically.
  • Archive Offline pages put away for good. An archived page leaves every count, list, chart, email and export and is not checked at all. Find them through Status → Archive or the Archive figure, and bring one back with Restore.

To clear out dead pages: filter Recently offline, select all, choose Archive.

11Leaving a note

Every page, document, board and update has a notes box at the bottom.

It is for the reasoning, not the fact, for example: “checked with marketing, the new subheadline is intentional, leaving it”.

Edit or delete your own whenever, mark one done once it is handled, and read everything anyone has written under Notes. That is also where you say something is broken, confusing or missing, in the box at the top.

12Export: getting it out as a spreadsheet

Download everything per page gives one workbook with every page and all of its content and owners, spelled out. Or build a narrower table: you pick the rows and the columns, and the number beside every option tells you how much you would get.

  • A row per page: which pages have which components, and who owns them. “Is this page missing a datasheet?”
  • A row per document, video or board: where each one is used. “Which datasheet is on the most pages, or on none?”
  • A row per page × document: every pairing, with the document’s full metadata as columns.

Columns include ownership (with the PL source and segment), status, every component, Perfect page ({% for ps in psets %}{{ ps.name }} {{ ps.presets[0][0] }}{{ ' / ' if not loop.last }}{% endfor %}), the SURF standard, Missing key components, and what the buy box, primary document, package link and meta description actually say. Paused and offline pages are marked in a State column rather than left out.

From the Dashboard, Export on ticked pages exports just those.

13What it emails

Two emails, both only to people who have Email alerts switched on in their profile. Nothing else is ever sent.

  • The report, when a check finishes. Every scheduled check sends it. A check started by hand sends it only if Send report was ticked. It gives the figures, what changed, and the queue.
  • A reminder on Friday at 08:00 (German time), only if updates or file updates are still waiting, so the queue is cleared before the next check reports on top of it. If the queue is empty, nothing is sent.

Missed one? Send latest report in your profile mails the last report to you alone.

14Settings: what you can change

{% if is_admin %}Settings is{% else %}Settings is admin-only, and is{% endif %} four tabs.

Schedule

It runs {{ every }} at {{ at_time }} today.

  • Repeat: on set days (tick the weekdays) or every N days. Weekly is the normal choice. Infineon does not republish most pages most days.
  • Start at: the hour, in your time. Overnight leaves the check the night to finish in.
  • Parallel browser tabs: how many pages are opened at once. Higher is faster, but pages start coming back half-read.
  • Captures kept per page: {{ s.keep_snapshots }} today. A comparison needs two.

Changing the schedule does not touch a check already running. The next run simply starts at the new time.

Sensitivity

Two numbers, both about the screenshot: how much of the picture has to differ ({{ s.visual_threshold }}% of pixels) and how far the page height has to move ({{ s.height_threshold }}px). Text, library and image changes are not thresholded. Raise them if pages keep coming up on the picture alone with nothing you can see.

Components

A grid of page types against components{% if is_admin %}, under Settings → Components{% endif %}. Every cell says how many captured pages of that type carry that component, and you tick what should be there.

  • Untick never-seen clears components that appear on zero pages of a type. Do it after a check finishes, not during one.
  • Pages with no level yet can be given one automatically from their siblings, their depth and their components.

Advanced

  • The shared link: a read-only link for people without an account. They cannot change anything, reach Settings or download the database.{% if is_admin %} Who used it shows under Manage people.{% endif %}
  • Copy limits: the longest a description, sub-headline, meta description, features, benefits or overview block may be, and how many a page should carry. A page over a limit is flagged beside the block, and the Dashboard’s Content filter lists who is over.
  • Where your assets live: the DAM, PIM, Windchill and AEM author addresses that make the badges one-click links.
  • Backup: a copy of the database, the part that cannot be rebuilt.

What you cannot change: the six signals, the double-check, the retry rounds, and the lines left out of the comparison. Those decide whether iRadar is right, and they are not knobs.

17Worth knowing

  • Only the last {{ s.keep_snapshots }} captures of a page are kept. Enough to compare and to show a before and after, not an archive.
    Anything an open update needs is never removed, however old.
  • Numbers never shift the list. The pager and the filter chips always sit in one row above the table, so filtering never pushes the rows down while you read.
  • Redirects are followed and merged. If two addresses land on the same page it is watched once. A ?tab= on the end is not a redirect, only the same page opening on a tab.
  • Videos count where they sit. Videos · On page, · News & highlights and · Training are separate components, so a page is not credited with a video that is only in its news strip.
  • The server’s disk is not backed up by anything else, so somebody has to keep a copy. {% if is_admin %}Download the database: it holds the page list, the ownership, every decision and every note.{% else %}An admin can download it from Settings.{% endif %}

18What it does not do

  • It does not find new pages by itself. iRadar watches the {{ '{:,}'.format(f.pages) }} pages it has been given. If Infineon publishes a new one, somebody has to add it{% if is_admin %} under Add pages{% endif %}.
  • A few finder pages only capture their first page of results.
  • Gated documents, the ones behind a MyInfineon login, cannot be previewed, because all we can see is the login wall.
  • It does not watch continuously. A change published and undone between two checks is a change nobody sees.
  • It does not chase anyone beyond two emails: the report and Friday’s reminder, to those who switched alerts on. The queue waits on the Dashboard until somebody opens it.
  • It reports; it does not judge. Whether a change matters is your call. iRadar only promises to notice it.
